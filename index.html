How much water, energy and carbon does AI use?

AI runs in data centres. They use electricity, and can use water for cooling and for making that electricity. This page adds up what one published measurement implies for your own use over a year.

These are estimates built from published studies, not measurements of your own use. Every number lists where it comes from.

    Prompt
    One question or request you type to an AI chatbot.
    Wh and kWh (electricity)
    A watt-hour (Wh) is a small amount of electricity: a 10 W LED bulb uses 10 Wh in an hour. 1,000 Wh make a kilowatt-hour (kWh), which is what home electricity bills use.
    Greenhouse gases and CO₂e
    Things we make or do release gases that warm the planet, such as carbon dioxide and methane. "CO₂e" (carbon dioxide equivalent) is one number that turns all of them into the amount of carbon dioxide that would warm the planet the same. It lets different things be compared.
    Blue, green and grey water
    Blue is water taken from rivers and underground. Green is rain that crops and grass soak up. Grey is the water needed to dilute pollution. Most of the water behind beef is green.
    Power-plant water
    Water that evaporates at power stations to make the electricity a data centre uses. It is separate from the water the data centre itself uses for cooling. Google's figure leaves it out; the older estimate includes it.
    Not counted
    Training the AI model and making its chips also use energy and water. The estimates here do not include them, because no published figure that fits was found.
    Squashed (logarithmic) scale
    A chart where each step along the bar means "ten times bigger" instead of "the same amount bigger". It makes small things visible, but bar lengths no longer match real sizes.
    Efficiency vs growth
    When each use gets cheaper, people may use more of it, so the total can still rise. Economists call this the Jevons paradox.
